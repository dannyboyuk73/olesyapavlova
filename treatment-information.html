Botulinum toxin: treatment information

This page provides balanced, factual information about botulinum toxin treatment for people who are attending or considering a consultation. It is not an advertisement or an offer of treatment.

Botulinum toxin is a prescription-only medicine (POM). By law it cannot be supplied without a prescription, and it can only be prescribed after a face-to-face medical consultation and assessment of your suitability by a qualified prescriber. It is never available to buy on demand.

The information below is provided so that you can make an informed decision and ask questions at your consultation. It is written for information only and is not intended to promote or advertise the medicine.

What it is

Botulinum toxin is a purified protein used in medicine for a range of purposes. In facial treatments, very small, targeted doses are used to temporarily relax specific muscles. It is supplied under several brand names and is a prescription-only medicine.

How it works

When injected into a muscle, botulinum toxin temporarily reduces that muscle's activity by blocking the nerve signals that make it contract. Where a muscle repeatedly creases the overlying skin, relaxing it can soften the associated lines. The effect is temporary.

What to expect

The effect is not immediate — it typically begins over the following days and develops over about two weeks. Results usually last in the region of three to four months, after which muscle activity gradually returns. Duration varies between individuals.

Areas it may be used

Depending on your assessment, treatment may relate to areas such as frown lines between the brows, horizontal forehead lines, lines at the outer corners of the eyes, and other specific concerns discussed at consultation. Which areas — if any — are appropriate is determined individually.

Possible risks and side effects

As with any medical procedure, there are possible side effects. These can include temporary redness, swelling, bruising or tenderness at the injection sites, headache, and — less commonly — temporary drooping of an eyelid or brow, or an uneven result while the effect settles. Serious complications are uncommon but possible. All risks are discussed with you before any treatment.

Who it may not be suitable for

Botulinum toxin is not suitable for everyone. It is not given during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or to people with certain neuromuscular conditions or allergies, among other factors. Your suitability is assessed individually as part of your medical consultation.

Aftercare

You will be given clear written aftercare and a point of contact for any questions. A review can be arranged to check how you have responded.
