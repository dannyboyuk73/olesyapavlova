The difference

Why choose a dentist for facial aesthetics?

In an unregulated market, who holds the needle matters more than anything else on the price list.

The uncomfortable truth about this industry

In the UK, dermal filler for cosmetic purposes can legally be injected by people with minimal training. The difference between practitioners is invisible on a price list — and only becomes visible when something needs judgement: an assessment that should end in "not yet", an unusual anatomy, or a complication that needs recognising in minutes, not days.

Five years of head and neck anatomy — then twenty of practice

A dentist's degree is a five-year clinical training built substantially on the anatomy of the head, face and neck — the vessels, nerves and muscle planes that facial aesthetics works within. It's followed, in Olesya's case, by more than 20 years of treating patients: assessing medical histories, administering thousands of precise facial injections, managing anxious patients gently, and working to surgical standards of hygiene and record-keeping every single day.

Most people never think of it this way, but a routine dental appointment is a facial injection appointment. Dentists give more facial injections, more often, into more varied anatomy, than any other profession — including doctors and nurses.

Accountability you can check

Olesya is registered with the General Dental Council — GDC No. 156643, which you can verify yourself on the public register. That registration means enforceable professional standards, mandatory indemnity insurance, continuing professional development, and a regulator with the power to act. A practitioner without professional registration offers none of that, whatever their Instagram looks like.

A medical assessment, not a sales pitch

A clinician's obligation is to the patient, not the transaction. That's why every treatment here begins with a £50 consultation — redeemed in full against your treatment — that includes a medical assessment of your face and your history, and sometimes ends with the honest advice that treatment isn't right for you. "Look fresher, not different" isn't a slogan; it's the clinical philosophy that decides what gets recommended and what doesn't.

What this means for your treatment

Careful candidacy decisions — including on higher-judgement areas like tear troughs and the nose. Precision placement from a professional whose entire career is precise work in small facial spaces. And prescription matters handled as the law requires: assessed face to face, never advertised, never sold off a menu.
